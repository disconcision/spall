FIDELITY · 1× VS 2×

One small piece of the city — a stepped back row (old buildings, the core, a capsule stack), a canal gap, a low front deck — drawn twice. 1× is the game's own render, captured from the running prototype. 2× is the same cells, the same sprite variants, the same palette and face shading, but every painter rewritten for a 32-px cell with the detail a doubled grid can hold. Nothing is upscaled or filtered.

Reading it

What 2× adds

  • Windows become windows: frames between runs, a mullion in each, a bright sill and its shadow, a slant of sky in the glass, blinds; lit ones get a warm top edge.
  • Material: render grain and weep stains on old walls; board-marked concrete with tie holes on the core; plank joints and a two-tone safety edge on decks.
  • Railings with rails: top rail, highlight, mid rail, posts — at 1× they are a dotted line.
  • Roofs carry things: parapets with coping, gravel, an AC unit with a fan, a tank on legs, a vent stack, a hatch.
  • Capsules read as moulded pods: rounded window with a rim and curtain, louvred vent, service hatch, rivets, bolted cage joints.
  • Cables become tubes: outline, highlight and a specular glint; the hose shows its ribbing; ties are lit bands that wrap.
  • Trees: a forked trunk and a lobed crown lit from one side; the conifer gets tiers with ragged hems.

What it costs

  • Every painter rewritten, not scaled: ~25 tile types plus facades, ghosts, cut (poché) faces and icons. This page did 6 of them; roughly 3–4× the art effort of the 1× set, and every new piece costs that from now on.
  • Memory: level caches are 4× bigger (depth, face and colour per pixel): ~5 MB a level now at 64×64 → ~20 MB; with lighting layers, ~0.5 GB for 18 levels. Tiling the caches becomes necessary, not optional.
  • Frame cost: 4× the pixels to composite and to light; the per-pixel cable drawing would need to move off fillRect.
  • The world reads smaller: at the same screen size you see a quarter of the area — or you keep the area and every element is half the screen size it is now.

Honest read

  • Up close, 2× wins clearly. Windows, railings and roof clutter become legible objects, and the cables gain the most: they read as tubes with weight, the hose has ribbing, ties read as bands that wrap.
  • At the default zoom it is a change of character more than of legibility. 2× reads as finer, calmer, more illustrated (eBoy / SimCity 3000 density); 1× reads bolder and more graphic, with a toy-like charm that is part of the current look.
  • The real decision is zoom. A 2× world shows a quarter of the area per screen at the same pixel size; seeing the whole block again means zooming out, which needs either a second (1×) art set or downsampling, and downsampled pixel art goes mushy. If most time is spent close (laying and tying cables), 2× pays for itself; if the overview is the game, 1× is the honest scale.
