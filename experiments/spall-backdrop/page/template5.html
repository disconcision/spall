Spall · concept · backdrop · round five

Night, sky, and merged in

Night is darker and navy, with fainter warm lights spread unevenly over the avenue grid, and the lit rectangles are gone. The 3D view has round one's pixel sky: a dithered gradient, the sun, a cumulus deck in perspective, and stars at night. The backdrop is no longer a separate experiment. It's part of the prototype now, in both the 2D game and the 3D study.

The bluish morning: is it there?

Yes, but only under the morning light; noon is still the grey one. Press T to step through morning, noon, evening and night. In this round, morning has a bit more blue in the haze, and its sky above the horizon runs from blue to a warm cream, after round one's. Compare the first two buttons below.

Merged

The backdrop is now stratum-wires/src/93_backdrop.js, built into stratum.html. The 3D study adds a small hook, spall-3d/src/92_backdrop3d.js, that draws the same world through its own camera. spall-backdrop no longer has a build: it keeps only the shot scripts and report pages. B still switches to the old flat sky, for now. Nothing is committed yet.

01 · NIGHT

Far off, so faint

The air, walls and haze are dark navy, and the lights are a warm yellow-orange. Far-off windows are fewer and dimmer points, and only one light in seven twinkles, and gently. The lit roof outlines are gone. Lights are spread unevenly: each 400-cell tile has its own share, and the avenues between tiles get more. Squint and you find a loose grid, but it shouldn't jump out. Street lamps and traffic are toned down to match.

Look for

  • Whether it still distracts from the city.
  • Whether the grid is too faint, about right, or overdone.
02 · SKY

Round one's sky, from the same eye

Above the horizon, the 3D view now paints round one's sky: a six-stop dithered gradient, the sun's disc, a cumulus deck about 2 km up (lit on its sun side, flattening and fading toward the horizon), and stars at night. It's drawn from the same eye as the undercity, so it turns with the view and doesn't swim. You see it when you tilt down to 12° (S) and zoom out a little. These shots are at 0.6×.

03 · 2D

The main view, four lights

The 2D view never shows the sky, but the haze carries each light's colour: blue-grey in the morning, grey at noon, orange in the evening, and navy at night.

My read

Night now sits behind the city instead of competing with it. The sky makes the low 3D angles feel like a place. The clouds read as thin streaks because we only ever see the deck at a low angle; a lower deck or a wider zoom-out would show more of it.
