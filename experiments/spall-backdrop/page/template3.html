Spall · concept · backdrop · round three

Lower, darker, and a second world

The pedestal is now shorter and darker, sits just inside the city's edge, and reads as the city's own lower body. The smog is no longer a noisy cloud blanket: the undercity shows through thinner, layered air. The horizon no longer stops: far geometry fogs into exactly the sky behind it, and giant towers rise through the haze to break the line. The undercity has more to it than plain boxes. And the dark grey-blue abyss from round one is back, rebuilt in the new model. Every image and clip here came out of the game.

Was there only one new backdrop?

Yes. Round two rebuilt only the undercity. Now there are two: Undercity and Abyss, the dark grey-blue foggy one you asked about. The other round-one ideas (the cloud sea, horizon cities, close neighbours) are still on Shift B in their old, swimming form. I'd drop them.

What I meant by “diffusion textures”

In round two you said a diffusion- or transformer-generated pass might come later, as long as it matches the game's low-fidelity pixel art. I meant using an image model to paint small facade and monolith textures, which the renderer would wrap onto these boxes. The geometry would keep the views agreeing, and the paint would add fidelity. It's parked: this round adds the detail as geometry instead.

The guide, in order

  1. The pedestal smaller than the city, darker, uneven, near featureless
  2. The undercity in 3D tilt down to the horizon; the clouds are gone
  3. The undercity from the main view finer grain, so it reads as far below
  4. The abyss round one's grey-blue void, in the new model
  5. What changed, and what's open
01 · PEDESTAL

Part of the city, not under it

It now starts a few cells inside the city's edge, so the city overhangs it like a lip. It's built from bays of different depths, some stopping partway down, with slots between them. The colour is the city's own darkest wall tone, and it's nearly featureless. Under the city's edge it falls into shadow by day, and the city's lights spill down onto it at night. It drops 420 levels (about 1.4 km) instead of 600, and at the bottom it splays into a footing among the undercity's streets.

Look for

  • Whether the city reads as the top of one mass, or as something set on a box.
  • Whether it's dark enough, or should go darker still.
02 · UNDERCITY · 3D

Down to the horizon

The orbit's tilt dips to 12°, which is where you saw the clouds and the hard horizon last time. Now the haze thins with height, so you see the city through it. Far geometry fogs to the same colour as the sky behind it, so there's no edge where the world stops. Nine giants 16 to 40 km out rise through the haze, and their tops break the horizon line.

Look for

  • The horizon: does it still feel fake?
  • The monolith in front: it's one of the near ones, about 4 km out. Is it too dominant?
03 · UNDERCITY · 2D

From the main view

The undercity's grain is now finer: superblocks are 100 cells rather than 150, cut into more blocks. With the shallower drop, that keeps it reading as far below rather than next door. Smog thickens gradually with depth, with no surface to it, so roofs come through clearly and streets sink into it. The clip scrolls from the pedestal up to the top of the city, then turns through all four rotations.

Look for

  • Scale: does the undercity feel far below?
  • Dusk is the haziest of the three. Day is the clearest.
04 · ABYSS

Inside the larger thing

Round one's grey-blue void, rebuilt in the new model. The city's pedestal drops into a void with no floor. Colossal pillars stand around it, each built from lengths of different girth with collars between them. Open trusses and draped cables span between neighbours, and a few pillars carry platforms like ours. The air thickens both ways: dark below, a pale glow above, so the pillars rise and sink out of sight with no visible top or bottom. To keep the views agreeing, no pillar near the city can rise high enough to cross in front of it at any angle.

Look for

  • Whether this is the mood you remembered.
  • It's sparse on purpose. The trusses and cables are faint; they could carry more.

My read

Undercity by day is the strongest image so far: misty, contiguous, monolithic, and the city clearly sits on something. Abyss by day has the mood but is thin on structure. Night works in both, but it's quiet. Dusk is the weakest: the orange smog swallows the most detail.

The pedestal is the change I'm least sure of. Near-featureless was your suggestion, and I think it's right, but the line where the city meets it is worth a close look in the 2D view.

What changed

  • Pedestal: inset 1.5–7 cells; bays and slots; the city's dark tone; a shadow lip and a night spill; a splayed footing; 420 levels, down from 600.
  • Air: one model for geometry and sky. Each pixel's air colour depends on its direction from the eye, and fog thins with height. The smog's top fades in over 150 levels instead of being a noisy surface. Far geometry ends in exactly the sky colour, so the horizon has no seam.
  • More than boxes: battered skirts, fins, ledges and crowns on the monoliths; pyramid caps and masts on towers; viaducts on piers down some avenues; trusses and cables in the abyss. Boxes can now taper.
  • Horizon: the city runs out to about 24 km (only plinths past 7 km), and nine giants stand among the far parts.
  • Size: about 100,000 boxes for the undercity and 116,000 for the abyss, generated in 60–110 ms. Each redraw takes 6–10 ms, and only when the camera moves.

Knobs

  • URL hash parameters, for example backdrop.html#bd=abyss&tod=dusk.
  • bd scene (under, abyss) · uz pedestal depth in levels (420) · ue perspective (150; lower is stronger) · uf haze distance · uh how fast haze thins with height · ufl smog density.

Open

  • The city's own mist should take the backdrop's air colour, so the city's edge sinks into the same air.
  • Abyss: heavier trusses, light falling in shafts from above, and more platforms with their own life.
  • Motion: traffic on the viaducts, aircraft lights, slow drifting haze.
  • Night needs more contrast in both scenes.
