Spall · concept · backdrop · round four

One backdrop, four lights

The undercity is now the only backdrop, and the city below is much less blocky. Each tile is split again and again, and each piece becomes low roofs, courts, rows of slabs, yards of sheds or towers. Large flat stretches carry fine geometric texture, and a few large things stand up out of them. There's a new morning light in the original sky's colours: a little blue up top and a warm cream low down. Night is darker, and its lights twinkle; cars move along the avenues and viaducts. Every image and clip here came out of the game.

Can we ever see the sky?

In the 2D view, no, and that's correct for the camera. It looks down at 30°, so the horizon sits about 980 art pixels above the centre of the view, well off the top of the screen. Bringing it on screen would take a perspective strong enough to warp the undercity badly. So the sky only appears in the 3D view, when you tilt down near level (S). The morning orbit below shows it.

The 3D sky is still simple: a gradient with faint stratus bands. A pixel-art sky like round one's first panorama could go there, and it would agree with the geometry, because it's drawn from the same eye.

What I cleaned up

The abyss is gone, and so are all of round one's panoramas. Their code and old shot scripts are in local/attic/, which isn't committed, so nothing is lost. The prototype is now one module, src/93_under.js.

01 · FABRIC

Less blocky

Three slow fields set the districts: how built up, how tall, and what sort of fabric. Some districts are carpets of low roofs and courts, some are rows of slabs and yards of sheds, and towers come in clusters. Within a district, each tile between the avenues is split again and again, and the streets narrow at each step. Each step may raise its ground on a podium, so neighbouring buildings share a level. The podium decks are darker, so the streets read between the roofs. Ten monoliths, 28 smaller landmarks in three forms (tiered, battered and stepped) and nine giants on the horizon stand up out of it.

Look for

  • Whether it's varied enough, vertically and in shape.
  • Whether the flat stretches read as textured, not blank.
02 · LIGHT

Morning, noon, evening, night

The same geometry under four lights, following the game's own light setting. Morning is the new one: a low warm sun, clearer air, and the original sky's palette, blue above and cream near the horizon. Noon is the dreary grey from before. Evening is unchanged apart from a little less smog. Night is darker: far-off windows are single bright points rather than a glow, a share of the lights twinkle, and lamps are scattered down the streets.

03 · MOTION

Morning orbit, and night held still

The orbit's tilt dips to 12°, where the sky shows. The night clip holds the camera still, so you can see the twinkle and the traffic. The backdrop redraws about nine times a second after dark, and only when the camera moves by day.

My read

The fabric is much better from the main view: it reads as a real city seen from far up, with stretches of fine texture and things standing up out of it. Morning is the friendliest of the four. Night is the most striking, though in motion its sparkle is busy, and it may want fewer points.

What changed

  • Generator: recursive splits under district fields replace the old regular grid. It makes about 230,000 boxes in about 0.3 s, once. Each redraw still takes about 8 ms.
  • Morning: a new palette with clearer air, picked up from the game's own morning light.
  • Night: darker walls and air, single bright points for far windows, twinkle, lamps in the streets, and moving traffic on the avenues and viaducts.
  • Removed: the abyss, the round-one panoramas and Shift B.

Knobs

Open

  • The city's own mist should take the backdrop's air colour.
  • Night density: fewer, brighter points might read better in motion.
