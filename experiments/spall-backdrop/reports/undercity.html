Spall Undercity
Spall · concept · backdrop · round two

A city on a pedestal

The city is now the top of a tower miles high. Below and around it lies an endless city of blocks built from blocks, sinking into haze, with a few monoliths standing up out of it. All of it is real geometry, drawn through the same camera as the city, so it turns, tilts and pans exactly as the city does in both views. Every image and clip here came out of the game.

Why round one swam, and the horizon question

Both views are orthographic: every ray is parallel. A painted sphere at infinity can't agree with that. In true ortho the sky would be one flat colour, so I faked a perspective lens for it, and the city never moved that way. That mismatch is the swim.

You're right about the horizon. With an ortho camera tilted down, every ray eventually hits an endless floor, so you never see a horizon or sky. You'd just see the floor fading into haze as it gets farther away. The other catch is that nothing in ortho shrinks with distance, so a city far below would look as big as ours.

So the city and the pedestal stay strictly ortho, and only what lies past the city's far side gets a gentle perspective: it shrinks toward the centre of the view, and slides less when you pan. The horizon then sits about 30° above the view's centre, far off-screen at the normal tilt, and only shows when you tilt the 3D camera down near level.

Where this stands

The agreement problem is solved by construction: there is no picture, only a world, and both cameras look at it. What's left is art direction. I think day is closest to what you described: abstract, contiguous, and mostly air. Night has the best bones (a lit grid, monoliths with galleries) but needs more and denser light.

The smoke plumes are gone. At this scale they'd be invisible.

How it is made

  • About 49,000 boxes: superblocks of blocks of lots and towers, ten tiered monoliths and a tiered pedestal, generated in about 40 ms. Heights are tied to footprints, so roofs dominate from above, the way a real city does from miles up.
  • One WebGL pass, about 12 ms, re-run only when the camera moves. The 2D view reads its camera straight off projIso, and the 3D view uses its own. In both, the city's far side marks where the perspective starts.
  • Facades come from the shader: windows, ribs, decks and lit galleries. Detail fades out once a storey is a pixel or two tall, so distance reads as calm haze, not noise.
  • Air: haze with distance, plus a smog layer the low city sinks into, all stepped and dithered with the game's Bayer pattern.

Next

  • Fidelity: the geometry and lighting are now right, so a diffusion or transformer pass could paint facade and monolith textures in the game's pixel style. The renderer would map them onto the boxes, keeping the agreement.
  • Give the city's own mist the backdrop's haze colour, so the city's edge sinks into the same air.
  • Design the pedestal properly: it's the most visible new thing, and it's a plain stepped column for now.
  • Make night denser, and add slow motion: traffic along the avenues, aircraft lights, and smog drifting.
