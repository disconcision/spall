What lies beyond the arcology

Five candidate worlds for the backdrop, each painted as a full 360° sphere and shown in the game as it is now: the 2D view scrolled up to the top of the city, and the 3D study from a few angles. The current sky

A flat gradient with two rows of grey skyline, fixed to the screen. It never moves with the city, and the 3D view shows the same picture whichever way it faces.

A

Alone

The arcology stands by itself on a dry salt flat. A far ridge, a few broken stumps, one ruin 5 km out and a fallen tower lying on its side. Big weather overhead. It makes the structure the last thing standing.

B

Cloud sea

The top deck is above the weather. A cloud sea 360 m down hides everything below it, and the tops of a dozen other towers break through, out to 70 km. Thin, clear air with cirrus overhead.

C

Horizon cities

Ours is one of many. Three rings of arcologies 9 to 80 km out and 1 to 6 km tall fade into smog. Great cables sag between neighbours. Below, the undercity glows up through the haze.

D

Close neighbours

The high-detail version: nine neighbours within 6 km, close enough to read their decks and lit windows, with skybridges and cable runs between them. The far ring stays hazy behind.

E

Inside the larger thing

We are not outside at all. Colossal pillars rise from an abyss to a coffered ceiling 2.6 km up. Trusses and cables cross between them, and light falls in shafts through a few openings. Mostly shadow and haze.

IN MOTION

Orbiting inside

A full turn of the 3D study with "Inside the larger thing" behind it. The sky is at infinity, so panning doesn't move it and orbiting turns it. Orbits for the other four are in local/vid/.

My pick

Horizon cities as the default. It works at all three times of day, gives the arcology a quiet band of fogged towers to stand against, and says "one of many" without crowding the play area.

Inside the larger thing is the most distinctive and the closest to BLAME!, but it contradicts the current lighting: there is no open sky for sun and sky light to come from. If you want it, the light should follow it, arriving in shafts through openings far above. Light wells would then be part of the fiction rather than a lighting trick.

Alone and Cloud sea are the same idea, the last thing standing. I would keep one. Close neighbours is the high-detail version you asked for; it is busy enough to compete with the cables, so I would hold it for a zoomed-out or establishing view.

How it is made

  • One 2560 × 1280 equirectangular panorama per backdrop and time of day: 7.1 px per degree, so it lands 1 : 1 on art pixels at the 2D zoom.
  • Painted procedurally at load, about 1 s each, in the game's palette with the old sky's 4 × 4 Bayer dither. Towers, beams and cables are placed in metres around the arcology and projected exactly, with a depth buffer, distance fog and ground haze, so cables sag and towers hide one another correctly.
  • 2D: a 1 : 1 crop, so pixels stay crisp. Each 90° turn of the view shows another quarter of the world. The horizon is held to the city's far corner at L15, so it scrolls with the city and appears as you scroll to the top.
  • 3D: sampled per art pixel through a lens that matches the 2D crop at the iso tilt and becomes true perspective as you tilt down. It is recomputed on the CPU when the camera moves, about 10 ms; it belongs in a shader.

Try it

  • experiments/spall-backdrop/backdrop.html. Press B to cycle the backdrops (and back to the current sky), 3 for the 3D view.
  • It reads stratum-wires and spall-3d unchanged and adds one module, src/92_backdrop.js. Nothing else is touched or committed.

Open issues

  • The scene's own mist doesn't take the backdrop's haze colour yet. Matching them would tie the city into the sky, inside most of all.
  • In 3D the sky crawls a little while orbiting (nearest sampling). Panning is clean.
  • Close neighbours and Inside are only a few km away, so they should move a little when you pan in 3D. A second, nearer layer with slight parallax would fix that.
  • Night lights still read a bit like noise at a distance.
  • Everything is static. Drifting clouds, shafts that move, and lights coming on at dusk are all cheap on top of this.
