Spall 3D, lines at rest

Two ways to keep the art's lines joined when the camera stops at an odd angle. cleanEdge keeps every pixel a colour from the art and redraws the stair-stepped diagonals as straight edges. Anti-aliasing renders each pixel from 3×3 samples and averages them. Both use the same sprites and need nothing per building type.

In the 3D view, D toggles cleanEdge and A cycles anti-aliasing off → 2× → 3× → 4×. Hold Z to see today's renderer while you hold it. O turns snap off so the camera stays at an odd angle.

The world is frozen at the game's default cut (L9), and only the rendering differs between frames. The white outlines round the cut tops are the same in every mode: they are drawn as real lines on top of the scene, so neither fix touches them. The dotted marks inside those tops, and the roof grids, are art, and those do change. Each one is a 480×330 art-pixel crop, one screen pixel per art pixel, which is how the game renders before scaling up. Drag any picture to pan; all panes move together. Side by side puts the chosen rendering beside today's. All four shows every 3D version at once. Differences greys the picture and marks in magenta every pixel that differs from today's. At the home view, 2D is the 2D game's own frame. Keys: 1–4 pick a rendering, ← → change the angle, and holding Space shows today's.

Why the lines break

Iso art draws a straight line as a staircase: two pixels across, one down. Neighbouring steps touch only at a corner. Today's renderer gives each screen pixel the colour of the art pixel under its centre. Turn the plane slightly and the screen grid no longer lines up with the art grid. Corners are the first thing a pixel centre misses, so the staircase falls apart into dashes. This is the roof-tile grid, the dotted marks on cut tops, and deck and rail edges. It happens at rest, not only while turning.

The two fixes

  • cleanEdge is torcado's shader (MIT), the usual tool for rotating pixel art cleanly. For the point being drawn, it reads the art pixels around it (21 of them) and looks for 45° and 2:1 staircases. Where it finds one, it decides which side of the straight edge the point is on and shows that side's art pixel. The output is always one of the art's own pixels, never a blend. I ported it into the plane shader with 2:1 slopes on and white as the colour that wins ties, as in the original. At the home view it changes nothing: 0 pixels differ from today's renderer.
  • Anti-aliasing renders the scene's colour again at 3× in each direction and averages each 3×3 block into one art pixel. Every edge in the scene gets partial coverage, so lines can't break, but edges come out in in-between colours that aren't in the art. It also changes the home view: about 5% of pixels, mostly where two faces of a cell meet and their tones now blend. Cables, people and the other overlays are drawn afterwards and stay crisp either way.
  • Both runs cleanEdge for each of the 3×3 samples. The edges are smoother still, and just as soft.

Cost, and how to fix it

cleanEdge as written is far too slow to leave on: 59 ms against 8. Profiling shows the 21 texture reads cost almost nothing. The cost is its decision logic, run three times for every fragment of every plane, including the many hidden behind others. Packing colours into integers already halved it from 127 ms.

Almost all of that logic depends only on the art, not on where in the pixel the point falls. Which staircase is present, which side wins, and which neighbour to show are fixed for each art pixel and each quarter of it. Only the final test against the edge line depends on the point. So the plan is to work those decisions out once per atlas pixel, when a sprite is added, and store them in a small texture. The plane shader then does one lookup and up to three line tests. That should bring cleanEdge close to today's cost, and it gives exactly the same picture.

AA is cheap here (+6 ms at 3×), because the scene shader is cheap. It is a question of look, not cost.

My read

cleanEdge is what you were asking for: continuous lines in the still image that look like the same game. It doesn't change the home view at all. Its changes elsewhere are small and land on the lines and edges that were breaking. AA fixes continuity too, but it trades the crisp pixel look for a softer one, and it alters the home view. It might suit the messier parts of the style, but as the default it reads as a different game. I'd make cleanEdge fast with the per-pixel precompute and turn it on by default. AA can stay as an option to judge in motion.
