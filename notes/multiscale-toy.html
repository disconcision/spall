How it works
  • Coarse, everywhere. Each year every block updates a few numbers: households drift toward better neighbours, jobs follow nearby labour, land value diffuses, and buildings get redeveloped, abandoned or burned. Those events edit the block's lot genome.
  • Lift. At about 250 px per block the block is instantiated. Buildings come from the genome and the seed; households and jobs are apportioned with a house-monotone divisor method, so the totals match exactly and a block that gains households never takes any away from a building.
  • Restrict. Zooming out sums the microstate back up. The ledger checks macro against Σ micro for every detailed block, every year.
  • Boundaries. When households cross between a detailed block and a coarse one, the detailed side picks who moves and the coarse side receives exactly those people.
  • Relax. A block you leave stays warm for three years, or until six other blocks need the cache, then its microstate is dropped. The genome and your memory records survive, so lifting it again gives the same streets and buildings, updated by what happened meanwhile.
  • Memory. Records gain salience while you look and fade by 15% a year while you don't. Faded records are forgotten; past 20 records the least salient goes first. Listed buildings never fade.

Keys: T advances a year, + and − zoom, F fits the city, arrow keys pan.
