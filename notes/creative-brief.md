# Stratum: working creative brief (v0)

A proposal to react to, not a decision. Written 2026-09-30 after the first day of research, prototypes and notes.

## One line

A city you grow at every scale, together with others. You direct at the scale you're looking at; agents and the simulation carry the rest, and time accretes into structure.

## Pillars

1. **Accretion.** Cities grow onto, into and over themselves, and their history stays visible as structure.
   - Layers are eras.
   - Integration details (decals) are the visible trace of real relationships between neighbours: footbridges where people actually cross, signage where commerce clusters, laundry where density and age meet.
   - In the SC2K version, your own SimCity 2000 city becomes the ground floor the arcology grows over.

2. **Scale is a verb.** Each scale has its own tools and concerns: room and capsule → building → block → megaframe and district → region-wide flows. Zooming changes what you can do, not only what you see.
   - Coarse-graining is delegation. When you zoom out, detail is handed to agents and the simulation; when you zoom in, you take it back.
   - This is the renormalization idea as a mechanic, not just an optimisation. It unifies the multi-scale simulation, continuous zoom and the embodied agents.

3. **Co-builders, not assistants.** Agents have bodies (cursor, gaze), attention, memory, and perhaps distinct roles and sensibilities: a surveyor, a builder crew, a historian who tells you what changed while you were away.
   - They work at the scales you aren't attending to, and everything they do is telegraphed, paced and legible.
   - Plans are editable scripts with holes.

4. **One language for plans, patterns and mods.** The agent's plan scripts, the player's blueprints and patterns, and requested mods are the same structured artifact at different levels of trust: place → pattern → new behaviour. This is the Hazel idea as the backbone of crafting, not just of agent output.

5. **Cast, not rendered.** A house look that is its own thing, stylized rather than realist.
   - Chamfered, faceted, cast-concrete forms; crisp pixel clusters; a restrained palette with warm light and saturated accents.
   - Infrastructure (pipes, conduits, transit, voids) as primary architecture.
   - A small family of grid-rational slopes (1:1, 1:2, 2:1) as the silhouette language for large diagonals.
   - Variety comes from states of one world — era, upkeep, climate, time of day — rather than unrelated themes. The gritty underground and the clean white one can be the same place in different decades, driven by the simulation.

6. **Legible at every scale.** From 10 m to 100 km, something always reads: silhouettes and light at the far end, rooms and people at the near end, with continuous zoom and cross-scale-consistent art.

## How the tracks fit

- **The sc2k version** is the proving ground for co-building (the agent interface, voice and pointing, pacing, agent-authored buildings) and for the ground-floor story. Keep its Maxis fidelity proportional to what we learn from it.
- **The broader Stratum version** is the real game: a layered, multi-scale engine with the house look above.
- **Shared layers:** the agent kit (context, plan language, pacing), voice, and the multi-scale simulation design.

## Open questions for you

- Is "you direct at your scale, co-builders carry the rest" the core loop you want, or should the player be able to do everything at every scale themselves?
- One house look with world states, or genuinely distinct style packs players choose between?
- Do agents have distinct personalities and roles, or one general co-builder?
- What is the game's pressure? A goal, a scarcity, a threat (runaway Builders?), or purely creative play?
